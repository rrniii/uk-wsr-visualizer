# Evidence register

Search and access date: 22 September 2026. IDs are stable within this knowledge base. The 25 records comprise 17 research articles, one discussion paper, three software papers and four developer documentation/source records. Evidence from another radar, objective or data level is used to design a test, not to import an accuracy claim.

## Closely relevant clutter, biological and validation studies

**P01 — Gourley, Tabary and Parent du Chatelet (2007). [A Fuzzy Logic Algorithm for the Separation of Precipitating from Nonprecipitating Echoes Using Polarimetric Radar Observations](https://journals.ametsoc.org/abstract/journals/atot/24/8/jtech2035_1.xml). DOI: 10.1175/JTECH2035.1.** C-band, locally estimated membership functions and weights; three case studies. Useful precedent for combining polarimetric evidence and calibrating to wavelength/site. Its precipitation-versus-nonprecipitation objective is insufficient for preserving biology. Access: publisher abstract and indexed example caption; full article not independently reviewed.

**P02 — Hubbert, Dixon and Ellis (2009). [Weather Radar Ground Clutter. Part II: Real-Time Identification and Filtering](https://doi.org/10.1175/2009JTECHA1160.1).** S-band examples demonstrate selective clutter identification and spectral filtering while protecting near-zero-velocity weather. Figure 4 and the zero-velocity examples are useful review references. CPA and spectral reconstruction require information not established as available in our archived moments. Access: primary article methods, examples and conclusions.

**P03 — Rennie, Illingworth and Dance (2010). [On differentiating ground clutter and insect echoes from Doppler weather radars using archived data](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/17547/). DOI: 10.5194/amtd-3-1843-2010.** UK archived reflectivity/velocity; persistent insect signals can compromise hit-frequency maps, motivating temporal velocity variability. Directly relevant to biological contamination of background training. Access: institutional abstract, discussion-paper methods and author response; Figures 1–4 visually inspected. The text/plot date is 29 June 2009; the Figure 1 caption says 29 July. Publication status: AMT Discussions; a final accepted AMT article was not verified. Treat as supporting evidence with that qualification.

**P04 — Dokter et al. (2011). [Bird migration flight altitudes studied by a network of operational weather radars](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2010.0116).** Operational C-band profiles were compared with a dedicated bird radar in the Netherlands, Belgium and France over migration campaigns. Supports checking retained biological reflectivity, altitude profiles and movement, not only gate appearance. Species composition, radar cross section and bird/insect separation limit transfer. Access: author-institution PDF abstract and primary full-text campaign methods. [Author copy](https://cdn.knmi.nl/system/data_center_publications/files/000/068/461/original/dokter2010rsif_bird_migration.pdf).

**P05 — Ivić, Curtis and Torres (2013). [Radial-Based Noise Power Estimation for Weather Radars](https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-13-00008.1).** Noise depends on the received environment and antenna direction; errors affect both sensitivity and moment estimates. Evaluated with simulated and real time-series data. Supports auditing radar/pulse/time sensitivity estimates and weak-echo losses. It does not validate estimating our noise floor from already computed dBZ. Access: publisher abstract and introduction.

**P06 — Hall, Rico-Ramirez and Krämer (2017). [Offshore wind turbine clutter characteristics and identification in operational C-band weather radar measurements](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2959).** Two UK operational radars and offshore wind farms; wind-farm signatures require attention distinct from ordinary land/sea clutter. Provides geographically relevant stress cases for rotating targets and coastal sectors. Access: publisher methods/results excerpts and institutional author-copy record. [Author copy](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/files/108428269/qj2959.pdf).

**P07 — Kilambi, Fabry and Meunier (2018). [A Simple and Effective Method for Separating Meteorological from Nonmeteorological Targets Using Dual-Polarization Data](https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-17-0175.1).** S- and C-band tests use a depolarization-ratio estimate from ZDR and RHOHV. The reported separation was 96%, approaching 99% with despeckling on their test cases. These are their results, not expected UK performance; classifying biology as nonmeteorological must not cause its deletion here. Access: primary paper abstract through an institutional PDF index; full PDF retrieval failed. [Institutional copy](https://farm.atmos.illinois.edu/contents/pubs_pdf/2018-Kilambi_etal-Method_for_separating_meteo_from_non-meteo_targets_using_dualpol_data.pdf).

**P08 — Nilsson et al. (2018). [Field validation of radar systems for monitoring bird migration](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.13174).** Co-located weather, dedicated bird, marine and tracking radars in southern Sweden. Useful examples of matching altitude, detection volume and temporal aggregation when checking migration intensity and movement. Different instruments sample different targets and volumes; none supplies universal gate truth. Access: primary methods, tables and discussion.

**P09 — Lin et al. (2019). [MistNet: Measuring historical bird migration in the US using archived weather radar data and convolutional neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13280).** Weak training labels from dual polarization were evaluated against separate human annotations, including geographic and historical tests. Figures 2–3 illustrate region segmentation. Useful design precedent for separating cheap preliminary interpretation from acceptance evidence. US S-band biology/precipitation separation does not validate UK clutter removal or taxonomic identification. Access: primary training/evaluation sections, result tables and captions.

**P10 — Overeem, Uijlenhoet and Leijnse (2020). [Full-Year Evaluation of Nonmeteorological Echo Removal with Dual-Polarization Fuzzy Logic for Two C-Band Radars in a Temperate Climate](https://www.knmi.nl/kennis-en-datacentrum/publicatie/full-year-evaluation-of-nonmeteorological-echo-removal-with-dual-polarization-fuzzy-logic-for-two-c-band-radars-in-a-temperate-climate). DOI: 10.1175/JTECH-D-19-0149.1.** Four-hour calibration followed by full-year, two-radar evaluation; reflectivity-exceedance, accumulation and gauge checks. Strongly relevant evaluation design for temperate C-band operations. Rain-gauge agreement does not demonstrate biological retention. Access: KNMI and TU Delft author-institution abstracts/metadata; full paper not retrieved, so detailed numerical performance is not reproduced.

**P11 — Gauthreaux and Diehl (2020). [Discrimination of Biological Scatterers in Polarimetric Weather Radar Data: Opportunities and Challenges](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70250307). DOI: 10.3390/rs12030545.** Multiple biological classes and rain were examined using six radar variables. More inclusive classes classified more reliably, with confusion among similar biological groups. Supports an initial biological/uncertain label without invented species certainty. Access: USGS original-author abstract and publication record, plus publisher indexed description; full paper not reviewed.

**P12 — Michelson et al. (2020). [Monitoring the impacts of weather radar data quality control for quantitative application at the continental scale](https://doi.org/10.1002/met.1929).** Independent METAR precipitation occurrence and contingency scores were used to compare quality-control versions over a month of North American data. Supports repeatable change monitoring beyond software uptime. Surface occurrence has spatial/vertical sampling limitations and does not assess biology. Access: primary methods and discussion.

**P13 — Husnoo, Darlington, Torres and Warde (2021). [A Neural Network Quality-Control Scheme for Improved Quantitative Precipitation Estimation Accuracy on the U.K. Weather Radar Network](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/31670/noaa_31670_DS1.pdf). DOI: 10.1175/JTECH-D-20-0120.1.** Weather/clutter signal mixtures and real events evaluate the choice of usable elevation and downstream QPE. Figure 1 illustrates the trade-off between clutter-filtered low elevation and uncontaminated higher elevation. Strong UK-specific methodological example; it concerns signal processing and rainfall, not validation of our archive mask. Access: NOAA-hosted article PDF text, abstract and methods.

**P14 — Jatau, Melnikov and Yu (2021). [A Machine Learning Approach for Classifying Bird and Insect Radar Echoes with S-Band Polarimetric Weather Radar](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/34122/noaa_34122_DS1.pdf). DOI: 10.1175/JTECH-D-20-0180.1.** Averaging, target aspect and several biological patterns are examined. Figure 4 documents strong aspect dependence of polarimetric signals, cautioning against one universal biological threshold. Their S-band classifier is not directly transferable to UK C-band or a reference for clutter removal. Access: original PDF methods, data split, abstract and captions; Figure 4 visually inspected.

**P15 — Schaper et al. (2022). [Radio frequency interference detection and mitigation in the DWD C-band weather radar network](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/15/6625/2022/). DOI: 10.5194/amt-15-6625-2022.** Operational moment-based RFI detection was checked with an independent Wi-Fi adapter on the radar receiver. Figure 1 shows interference superposed on weather and its quality-variable signatures. Supports independent confirmation and azimuth/time tracking. Do not assume their pulse-based variability field exists in our archive. Access: primary article abstract and methods; Figure 1 visually inspected.

**P16 — Zhang, Li, Moisseev and Leskinen (2025). [A hybrid algorithm for ship clutter identification in pulse compression polarimetric radar observations](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/18/4839/2025/). DOI: 10.5194/amt-18-4839-2025.** Distinguishes ship mainlobes and sidelobes in C-band pulse-compression observations. Its binary ship-versus-precipitation setting omits the biology relevant here. Useful example for target-specific validation; the UK LP/SP labels do not establish matching waveform physics. Access: primary abstract and methodological context. Published 26 September 2025.

**P17 — Specht, Ośródka, Szturc and Freda (2026). [Removal of RLAN Interference from C-Band Weather Radar Data: Algorithm and Case Studies](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/15/2625). DOI: 10.3390/rs18152625.** Combines geometric and polarimetric information, tests interference overlapping precipitation and uses independent synoptic reports. Correction includes interpolation: these estimated values must remain distinguishable from observations in our application. Access: publisher indexed methods/conclusions and version record. Published 6 August 2026, before this review's cutoff.

**P18 — Ośródka and Szturc (2022). [Improvement in algorithms for quality control of weather radar data (RADVOL-QC system)](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/15/261/2022/). DOI: 10.5194/amt-15-261-2022.** An operational chain explicitly addresses wind turbines, terrain obstacles and different interference patterns. Supports class-specific diagnostics and evaluating the whole chain after combining components. Its meteorological objective treats biology differently from ours. Access: primary abstract, system description and scope.

## Software provenance: capabilities are not comparative accuracy

**S01 — Figueras i Ventura et al. (2020). [Pyrad: A Real-Time Weather Radar Data Processing Framework Based on Py-ART](https://doi.org/10.5334/jors.330).** Describes reproducible configurable processing, including moments and richer signal inputs. It does not describe or validate our custom `echo-clean-v3` logic. Access: original software-paper indexed text. [Publisher PDF](https://openresearchsoftware.metajnl.com/articles/330/files/submission/proof/330-1-4613-1-10-20201008.pdf).

**S02 — Michelson et al. (2018). [BALTRAD Advanced Weather Radar Networking](https://doi.org/10.5334/jors.193).** Documents an operational data exchange and processing toolbox and quality-plugin architecture. Evidence of system maturity, not performance of our new compound mask. Access: original paper indexed text and publisher bibliographic record.

**S03 — Dixon et al. (2026). [The Lidar Radar Open Software Environment (LROSE): An End-to-End Suite of Applications for Radar and Lidar Processing](https://www.eol.ucar.edu/opensky-publications/articles%3A46155/the-lidar-radar-open-software-environment-lrose-an-end-to-end-suite-of-applications-for-radar-and-lidar-processing). DOI: 10.5334/jors.630.** Describes the software suite and real-time/archive workflows. Does not certify this installation's flag conventions or biological retention. Access: NCAR original-author publication record and abstract; publication date 16 April 2026.

## Developer sources used to interpret our outputs

**D01 — SMHI/BALTRAD. [Polarimetric Processing Chain documentation](https://baltrad.github.io/baltrad-ppc/).** Documents fuzzy clutter processing followed by residual filtering, phase processing and attenuation. Some description of `clutterID` is ambiguous; executable source and runtime probes were used to resolve the relevant direction. The linked Vulpiani report returned 404 and was not read. Access: full developer page, API and option descriptions.

**D02 — BALTRAD PPC. [pdp_processor.c at commit 831190ec8c0ead27c72a9f672de0f9b30c1e252b](https://github.com/baltrad/baltrad-ppc/blob/831190ec8c0ead27c72a9f672de0f9b30c1e252b/ppc/pdp_processor.c).** The source subtracts clutter membership from one to form quality, and rejects residual-mask zero/nodata while retaining one. Directly relevant to the local wrapper and policy mismatch. The local installed primitive was independently probed for residual-mask behaviour. Access: original C source. This upstream commit is not asserted to be the installed binary's build revision.

**D03 — Finnish Meteorological Institute. [RACK Anomaly Detection and Removal](https://fmidev.github.io/rack/andrepage.html).** Distinct detectors address line-like emitters, broad jamming, ships and specks; the developers also distinguish deletion, damping and gap filling. Our intersections and excluded biological operators are local policy choices. Access: full developer page and detector descriptions. Documentation was retrieved on the research date; it is not a frozen specification of every installed release.

**D04 — NCAR. [RadxPid application documentation](https://github.com/NCAR/lrose-core/blob/master/docs/apps/radx/dualpol/RadxPid.md).** PID interest functions and thresholds depend on wavelength and transmit mode. Supports checking C-band simultaneous-transmit settings and using absent fields honestly. Access: developer application and parameter documentation; [parameter reference](https://ncar.github.io/lrose-core/docs/apps/radx/dualpol/RadxPidParams.html).

## Search record and inclusion decisions

Queries combined the four package names with clutter, noise, background, comparison, polarimetric preprocessing and validation; additional groups covered C-band/UK insects, birds, mixed echoes, zero-velocity weather, receiver-noise estimation, RLAN interference, sea clutter, ships, wind farms and independent radar validation. Citation following led from framework papers and developer references to original algorithm studies.

Priority went to UK or temperate European C-band studies, independent measurement comparisons, explicit preservation/error analysis and operational evidence. S-band and pulse-compression work was retained where it supplies a useful test or warns against transfer. Ground-penetrating, automotive, over-the-horizon and SAR clutter papers were excluded as unsuitable for these inputs. A recent taxonomic systematic review was used only as a discovery lead, not as primary evidence for a technical claim. No numerical scores were pooled across papers with different tasks, bands, labels or denominators.

Some primary text was available through the search index when direct fetching failed. Such access is distinguished from reading an entire article above. This register deliberately does not claim exhaustive database coverage or that all 25 sources were read cover to cover. No missing full text was replaced with an invented result.
